Step‑by‑step procurement playbook for tiles on Saudi hospitality giga‑projects: SABER/SASO compliance, CoC and pre‑shipment checks, phased delivery, lot continuity, storage and RFQ

This playbook turns regulatory complexity and phased logistics into a procurement‑ready plan for tiles on Saudi Arabia’s hospitality giga‑projects. It is written for architects, contractors, developers, importers, distributors and hotel procurement teams who must deliver specification‑grade ceramic and porcelain tiles on tight milestone schedules while meeting SABER/SASO conformity and customs clearance requirements.

Read it to prepare specification language, build an RFQ that suppliers can price without assumptions, and create a phased delivery and lot continuity plan that prevents customs delays and site rejections. Where rules or fees can change, the guide flags verification points so your team can confirm the latest SABER portal guidance and Conformity Assessment Body requirements before shipment.

Why a Saudi‑specific tile playbook matters for hospitality giga‑projects

Saudi projects are different: multiple opening milestones, large single‑source tile volumes, brand quality expectations and often strict Project Management Consultant (PMC) acceptance criteria. These projects combine high volume with narrow acceptance tolerances for shade, slip resistance and rectification between lots.

For procurement teams that treat tiles as commoditised items, the result is avoidable rejections, holdbacks at customs and disruptive last‑minute retesting. A playbook aligned to SABER/SASO compliance and phased logistics reduces that risk and converts specification requirements into executable RFQs and delivery plans.

SABER / SASO compliance: the non‑negotiables

Tiles for Saudi import typically fall under regulated product lists requiring registration and a CoC (Shipment Certificate of Conformity). Product registration, factory documentation and valid test reports must be in place before clearance. Where test methods, necessary parameters and HS code interpretations can change, always verify against the SABER portal and an authorised Conformity Assessment Body prior to shipment.

Plan for review time: document checks, possible sample testing and factory audits (if requested) are common. Suppliers with existing, dated lab reports should confirm validity windows with the Conformity Assessment Body and provide date‑stamped copies in the RFQ package.

Product registration, CoC and pre‑shipment workflow (spec‑ready)

1) Pre‑qualification pack: technical data sheet (TDS), batch/firing photos, PTV/ slip reports where relevant, material safety data sheet, shade and thickness tolerances, and existing test reports with lab accreditation noted.

2) Product registration: register the product on the SABER platform or appoint an authorised agent. The export pack must match the registered model numbers and declared HS codes to avoid reclassification delays.

3) Pre‑shipment Certificate of Conformity (SCoC): coordinate CoC timing with the shipment schedule — CoC is typically required before final loading for regulated categories. Confirm whether a Pre‑Shipment Inspection is mandatory for your shipments and who will arrange it (exporter, importer or appointed third‑party).

Pre‑shipment inspection and QC: sample, batch and test strategy

Pre‑shipment inspection should combine documentary checks and a physical lot review: verify glaze/porcelain body, shade mapping across panels, edge rectification, dimensional tolerances and water absorption where specified. Keep the same acceptance criteria used in the RFQ and contract.

For slip resistance and specialty finishes used in hospitality (pool surrounds, wet areas), include lab‑slip test results (PTV or equivalent) with the lot shipment and specify any in‑country re‑testing or appointed lab requirements in the contract to avoid surprises at handover.

Phased delivery & lot continuity: operational rules to prevent mismatch

Large hospitality projects usually accept deliveries in phases tied to fit‑out milestones. Establish lot continuity rules: minimum lot coverage per phase, reserved buffer lots, and an approved shade quota. Require suppliers to segregate and label lots by production batch and to provide lot‑by‑lot certificates tied to CoC documentation.

Include language for ‘make‑good’ and approved equivalents if the exact lot is unavailable — but also require final client sign‑off for any substitution. For critical visible areas, specify a single‑lot installation approach and reserve enough installed area to permit visual continuity checks during trial installations.

Customs clearance, freight and incoterms practicalities

Declare HS codes and product descriptions exactly as registered. Mismatched descriptions between invoice, packing list and SABER registration are a common cause of holdbacks. Specify who is responsible for CoC fees, pre‑shipment inspection and any in‑country testing in the contract and RFQ.

For project reliability, favour DAP or CFR with staged handover points aligned to project milestones and clear responsibility for demurrage and storage if shipments are delayed. Ask suppliers to provide load plans, pallet‑level marking and a bill‑of‑materials mapping to installation areas to speed customs checks and site acceptance.

On‑site storage, handling and traceability for hospitality projects

Designate controlled storage zones with humidity and stacking controls to prevent shading and glaze damage. Require supplier lot tags that remain with pallets until installation and a digital lot register for the project manager. For long‑lead projects, rotate buffer stock and use FIFO for lot release.

Implement a site acceptance checklist that captures lot numbers, visual match confirmation by an appointed inspector, and signed acceptance certificates before tiles are released to installers. This prevents rework and protects warranty claims tied to specific production lots.

RFQ and specification template: what to include to get actionable bids

Provide an RFQ pack that includes: nominated product code and photo, TDS, expected lot sizes and phased delivery schedule, mandatory test reports (with dates and lab accreditation), required CoC condition and incoterm, and a clear penalty/acceptance regime for mismatched lots.

Request explicit lead times for product registration and CoC issuance, sample lead time and sample cost, pre‑shipment inspection party and an itemised quote for logistics, inspection and registration fees. Ask suppliers to confirm capacity for staged releases and to submit a sample pallet photo and production plan with their offer.

Decision checklist

  • Confirm SABER product category and register product model/HS code before shipment.
  • Include date‑stamped accredited lab test reports (PTV, water absorption, bending strength if specified) in the RFQ.
  • Specify phased delivery schedule with lot continuity rules and reserve buffer lots.
  • Define incoterm and responsibility for CoC, pre‑shipment inspection and demurrage.
  • Require pallet‑level labelling, digital lot register and site acceptance checklist.
  • Request supplier confirmation of CoC timing and whether a factory audit or additional testing may be required.
  • Request sample pallets and photo documentation before bulk release; include sample approval time in the contract.
  • Include a defined dispute and substitution approval process with client sign‑off for any lot changes.

Frequently asked questions

Is SABER registration required for every tile shipment to Saudi Arabia?

If your tiles fall under regulated categories listed by the SABER/SASO programme, product registration and a Certificate of Conformity are typically required prior to clearance. Because lists and processes can change, verify current obligations on the SABER portal or with an authorised Conformity Assessment Body before you ship.

Who arranges the pre‑shipment inspection and pays for the CoC?

This must be clarified in the contract or RFQ. Either exporter, importer or an appointed third‑party can arrange inspection and CoC; costs and timing should be itemised in supplier quotations. For project risk control, many owners require the supplier to manage CoC timing aligned to the final loading schedule.

How do we prevent shade mismatch across phased deliveries?

Require suppliers to segregate and label lots by production batch, submit shade maps and pallet photos, reserve buffer lots and specify single‑lot installation for critical visible areas. Include acceptance criteria and a trial installation clause in the contract to validate visual continuity before bulk installation.

Can existing lab reports speed up SABER registration?

Yes — valid, accredited lab reports reduce the likelihood of additional testing, but their acceptance depends on date validity and test scope. Confirm with the Conformity Assessment Body whether your reports meet current SABER requirements and whether they require in‑country re‑testing.
