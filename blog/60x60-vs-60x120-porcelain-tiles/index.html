60x60 vs 60x120 Porcelain Tiles for Wholesale and Projects

A B2B decision framework for application, layout, handling, packing and replacement planning.

Home›Blog›60x60 vs 60x120 Porcelain Tiles for Wholesale and Projects
By LuxeCeram Export TeamPublished 2026-07-26 · Updated 2026-08-04

For broad wholesale demand and easier handling, 60x60 is often the more flexible starting format. For larger visual fields and premium project concepts, 60x120 may be the stronger design choice, provided the buyer has confirmed layout, handling, cutting, packing and installer capability. This is not a product-performance promise: the final decision must use the exact tile data, approved sample, project drawings and destination requirements.

Quick decision: when each format is easier to justify

Choose 60x60 when the commercial goal is a versatile assortment, easier handling across showrooms and sites, simpler replacement planning, or a format that can serve many medium-size rooms. Choose 60x120 when the project specifically values a more elongated module, fewer visible joints in the design direction, feature walls or larger open areas, and the installer and logistics plan can support the format.

Neither format is automatically “better.” A polished 60x60 and a matt 60x120 can serve very different applications, and the same nominal size can be offered with different thicknesses, edges, finishes, shades and technical values. Compare exact product codes, not size names alone.

Match the format to the room module and application

Start with a scaled layout, not a mood image. Record the room dimensions, fixed openings, columns, sanitary fixtures, movement joints and the preferred direction of the pattern. Then compare how many full tiles, narrow cuts and interrupted joints each format creates. A larger unit can look clean in an open space but create awkward cuts in a small or highly divided room.

For wall applications, check the elevation and the relationship between tile joints, doors, niches, counters and fittings. For floors, coordinate the grid with thresholds, drains and transitions. The designer or installer should confirm substrate, adhesive system, movement accommodation and installation method for the exact tile and site.

Wholesale buyers should separate project-led demand from general showroom demand. A 60x120 design may create strong visual impact but turn slowly in a market that mainly serves small rooms or contractors without large-format equipment. A balanced range can include core 60x60 options and selected 60x120 designs supported by samples and clear technical information.

Confirm handling, packing, warehouse and delivery capability

Do not estimate handling from nominal size alone. Ask for the actual pieces per carton, square metres per carton, carton weight, cartons per pallet, pallet dimensions, gross pallet weight and loading plan for the offered product. Verify whether pallets can be moved through the buyer’s warehouse and whether the destination site has suitable access and unloading equipment.

For 60x120, define who will lift, carry, cut and place the tile and what equipment is available. Edge exposure, long cuts and transfers between truck, warehouse and site deserve a written method. For 60x60, handling may be more familiar, but damage control still depends on pallet condition, stacking, strapping, corner protection and disciplined unloading.

If the order mixes formats, ask the supplier to show the proposed pallet and container plan before approval. Confirm whether a pallet contains one product, one shade/calibre batch and one label set, and how partial quantities are identified. Mixed loading should not be assumed practical until dimensions, weights and unloading sequence have been checked.

Documents and evidence to request before approval

The quotation and proforma invoice should identify each product code, nominal and working size where applicable, body type, finish, edge, thickness, quantity, packing, delivery term and exclusions. Ask for current technical data and test information that applies to the offered product, not a generic catalogue statement. Ceramic-tile definitions, classification, characteristics and marking are addressed internationally by ISO 13006, while project specifications may also refer to other national standards. The buyer must state which evidence the contract requires.

Approve a traceable sample or sample panel and record its code, shade, calibre, finish and approval date. Photographs can support the record but should not replace the physical sample where the transaction requires one. For a project, connect the approved sample to the material schedule and inspection criteria.

Request a packing list format, pallet data, label example, inspection scope and the transaction documents required for the chosen route. Customs, conformity, tax and licensing requirements vary by destination and change over time; the importer should confirm them with qualified local professionals before shipment.

A practical buying workflow

1. Define the decision

State application, destination, room modules, target design, quantity by item, acceptable alternatives and whether the purchase is wholesale stock or a named project.

2. Compare layouts

Prepare simple floor or wall layouts for both formats and identify full tiles, cuts, joints, waste assumptions and installation constraints.

3. Request matched offers

Send the same specification and RFQ to shortlisted suppliers. Require product-specific packing, technical and commercial information.

4. Approve samples and evidence

Record sample identity, finish, shade/calibre information, technical documents and any mock-up result required by the project.

5. Confirm logistics and inspection

Approve pallet and loading assumptions, unloading method, inspection points, document set and responsibility for deviations.

6. Protect continuity

Define extra quantity or replacement-stock policy by product and batch. Record how later replacements will be identified and what differences may be unavoidable.

How to compare supplier offers fairly

Normalise the offer before comparing price. Put both formats on one sheet with the same quantity basis, packing scope, delivery term, sample cost, inspection scope, origin transport, freight assumptions and exclusions. A lower unit price can be misleading when the carton, pallet, inspection or delivery scope is different.

Record every clarification in writing. When a supplier proposes a different thickness, edge, finish, packing method or mixed-loading plan, treat it as a new commercial version and request a revised quotation. Do not rely on chat messages that are not reflected in the final proforma invoice or product schedule.

Risk controls for 60x60 and 60x120 orders

  • Selecting from rendered images without a traceable sample.
  • Assuming one design, shade or technical value is identical in both sizes.
  • Approving 60x120 before confirming site access, lifting and cutting capability.
  • Comparing quotations with different packing or delivery scopes.
  • Mixing batches without a written shade/calibre and replacement plan.
  • Ordering project quantity before the layout and waste method are approved.

RFQ checklist

  • Buyer company, destination and delivery location.
  • Wholesale stock or named project; application and room types.
  • Exact product codes or requested design direction for 60x60 and/or 60x120.
  • Finish, edge, thickness, body and technical requirements to be evidenced.
  • Quantity by item and acceptable alternatives.
  • Sample, mock-up and approval process.
  • Carton and pallet data, label language and warehouse limits.
  • Mixed-loading request and unloading sequence.
  • Inspection scope, document set, delivery term and target timing.
  • Replacement stock, shade/calibre continuity and deviation procedure.

Frequently asked questions

Is 60x120 always more premium than 60x60?

No. Premium positioning depends on design, finish, quality, application, installation and market demand. Size alone does not establish product quality.

Is 60x60 always easier to install?

It is often more familiar to handlers and installers, but installation still depends on substrate, product characteristics, layout and the chosen system. The installer must approve the method for the exact site.

Does 60x120 reduce grout lines?

For the same covered area, a larger tile can create fewer joints, but the visual result depends on the layout, joint width, cuts and room geometry. Movement and installation joints must not be removed for appearance.

Which format is better for a mixed container?

The better option is the one supported by a verified pallet and loading plan, market demand and unloading capability. Ask for dimensions and weights before assuming the formats can be mixed efficiently.

What should be approved before payment?

At minimum: supplier identity, product schedule, commercial terms, sample process, packing assumptions, inspection scope, required documents and beneficiary details. The exact sequence depends on the transaction and risk level.
